If the cascaded convolutional decoder network is trained on natural images, then the decoder network is less likely to learn high-frequency components. Previous studies (Ruderman, 1994) have empirically found that natural images were dominated by low-frequency components. Specifically, frequency spectrums of natural images follow a Power-law distribution. I.e., low-frequency components (e.g., the frequency component $[u,v]$ closed to $[0,0]$, $[0,N-1]$, $[M-1,0]$, and $[M-1,N-1]$) have much larger length $\|\mathbf g^{(uv)}\|_2=\sqrt{\sum_c|G^{(c)}_{uv}|^2}$ than other frequency components. Besides, according to rules of the forward propagation in Equation (4) and the change of $T^{(l,uv)}$ in Equation (5), if the frequency component $\mathbf g^{(uv)}$ of the input image has a large magnitude, then $\mathbf h^{(uv)}$ of the output image also has a large magnitude. This means that using natural images as the input strengthens the trend of encoding low-frequency components.

Therefore, according to Corollaries 3.3 and 3.4, we know that if the cascaded convolutional decoder is trained on natural images, then the decoder is more likely to strengthen low-frequencies.
